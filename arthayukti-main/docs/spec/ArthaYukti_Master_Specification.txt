====================================================================== 1.
PRODUCT VISION AND PROBLEM TO SOLVE
======================================================================

ArthaYukti is a serious personal-finance planning and multi-goal
financial decision-support application. It is not merely an expense
tracker, investment dashboard, or chatbot.

The central problem is: what happens when a household does not have
enough money to achieve every financial goal on time? The application
must use the user’s complete financial picture to calculate which goals
can be achieved, which goals are delayed or underfunded, what changes
could improve feasibility, and how alternative assumptions change the
outcome.

Users can enter income, expenditure, financial goals, assets,
investments, debts, insurance, household profile information,
assumptions, and future events. The engine projects finances over time
and explains trade-offs. It must not double-count money or make
lower-priority goals appear feasible by silently violating
higher-priority commitments.

Core product promise: - Show the current financial position and future
trajectory. - Estimate whether goals can be achieved under explicit
assumptions. - Explain shortfalls and trade-offs. - Allow users to test
changes in independent scenarios. - Use AI to analyse the complete
relevant financial context, explain results, and propose options. - Keep
authoritative financial calculations in deterministic, testable code,
not in the language model.

2. NEW REQUIREMENT: HOUSEHOLD PROFILE, LIFE STAGE, AND RISK CAPACITY
======================================================================

The user profile must include structured information about age,
household composition, dependants, future family commitments, and
household income. Gemini must consider these factors together with all
financial data when analysing risk capacity and making suggestions
relative to goals.

Create a dedicated Household Profile / Life Context section. Do not
assume every user has the same family structure. The profile must
support user-selected answers and explicit “prefer not to say” or
“unknown” states where appropriate. Do not infer family members,
dependants, or children from names, transactions, or other sensitive
signals.

Suggested schema and UX fields: - User date of birth or age; prefer date
of birth stored securely and derive age as of the projection date. If
only age is supplied, record age, reference date, and whether it is
approximate. - Household arrangement / living situation only if useful
to the financial model and voluntarily provided. - Parent commitments:
whether the user financially supports or expects to support parents; use
Yes / No / Unsure, with optional expected monthly/annual support,
current health/medical-support budget if user chooses to provide it,
start date, expected duration or review date, and notes. Do not require
health details to use the feature. - Children: whether the user
currently has children; if yes, number of children and optional age/date
of birth per child, financial responsibility, education-related goals
and expected timing. - Future children: whether the user expects or
plans children in the next 10 years; if yes, allow the user to enter an
expected number and approximate expected year/range for each planned
child. The user explicitly requested “if yes, how many in the next 10
years.” Treat these as uncertain plans, not confirmed facts. Let the
user add, edit, disable, or mark as uncertain. Do not assume a planned
child will necessarily exist; use only in the scenario/assumption chosen
by the user. - Other dependants / family members financially supported:
count, relationship category, expected support amount, start/end date or
recurrence, and optional notes. Avoid unnecessary personally identifying
details. - Household income: model all income sources in the Income
module. Provide a clear household total based on selected included
income sources, with individual income amount, frequency, start date,
optional end date, growth schedule, reliability/variability and source
category. Do not create a separate family-member-management system;
income sources can be entered independently. A spouse/partner
contribution, if applicable, can be represented as another income
source. The household profile can record high-level context, while
detailed income sources remain in the Income module. - Household
financial commitments: optional expected family events/expenses,
recurring support, education costs, medical support, caregiving, and
other planned obligations. Represent actual expected amounts and dates
as cash-flow events/expense schedules rather than vague profile text
alone. - Profile completeness and provenance: user-entered, imported,
inferred-by-user-confirmation, last-updated time, and whether
assumptions are confirmed or uncertain.

Risk analysis: - Distinguish risk tolerance, risk capacity, and risk
need. Do not collapse them into one “risk score.” - Risk capacity means
the financial ability to withstand losses or income shocks without
jeopardizing essential spending, mandatory payments, emergency reserves,
dependants, and high-priority goals. - Risk tolerance is the user’s
willingness to accept volatility, gathered separately and only if the
product includes it. - Risk need is the return/risk level that might be
needed to reach goals; do not use it to justify unrealistic or
unsuitable recommendations. - Gemini should analyse age/life stage,
parents/dependants, existing and planned children, household income
sources and reliability, essential expenses, emergency-fund coverage,
debts and EMIs, insurance, investment liquidity/concentration, goal
priorities and deadlines, future family commitments, and scenario
sensitivity. - The deterministic engine should calculate relevant
quantitative indicators (e.g. months of essential expenditure covered,
debt-payment burden, income concentration if enough data, projected
cash-flow surplus/deficit, shortfall under adverse scenarios, liquidity
available for goals). Gemini interprets these indicators and explains
trade-offs. - Do not let Gemini assign a definitive risk capacity using
opaque intuition. Provide a transparent framework, factors,
thresholds/configuration, evidence and caveats. Label qualitative
assessments as estimates. - A suggestion to increase investment risk
must be checked against the household’s risk capacity, emergency
reserve, liquidity needs, debt obligations, time horizon and goal
priority. Higher expected returns must never be presented as
guaranteed. - Future family plans must be scenario-based. A planned
child, possible caregiving cost, or uncertain income event should not
automatically be treated as confirmed fact in the base plan. Show base
and alternative scenarios where useful. - Respect privacy: only provide
Gemini the relevant structured summary necessary for analysis. Do not
send unnecessary identifiers or raw sensitive data. Tell the user what
information is used for a risk-capacity analysis.

3. SOURCE-OF-TRUTH AND ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

Use a modular architecture with clearly separated responsibilities: 1.
Frontend/UI: onboarding, data entry, dashboard, module pages,
trajectories, charts, scenario tabs, import review, profile and reports.
2. Backend/API: authentication/authorization, validation, business
workflows, CRUD, audit trail, import staging, scenario management and
calculation requests. 3. Deterministic financial engine: event-dated
monthly projections, balances, investment growth, taxes, debt schedules,
goal allocation, liquidity and trajectory calculations. 4. Data layer:
normalized records, transaction ledger, scenario assumptions, external
market snapshots, calculation runs, audit history. 5. AI orchestration
layer: Gemini for contextual analysis, explanation, categorization
proposals, scenario ideas and optional research. Gemini does not
authoritatively calculate balances, taxes, returns, or goal feasibility.
6. External-data adapters: stock-market data, mutual-fund NAV sources,
and optional external research. These do not connect to personal
accounts.

Prefer modular interfaces and independently testable services. Do not
over-engineer distributed services for a 30-hour hackathon. A modular
monolith is a reasonable default unless the actual stack/project context
strongly argues otherwise. Choose a practical stack compatible with the
existing project repository if one exists; inspect it before proposing
replacements. If no stack exists, select a stack that is quick to build,
maintainable, and testable, and explain the choice.

Every projection must be reproducible from: - base financial record
version/snapshot, - selected scenario, - calculation-engine version, -
tax-rule configuration version and effective financial year, - market
data snapshot and timestamps where relevant, - projection start date,
end date and time-step, - all assumptions and user overrides. Store
enough metadata to explain why a result changed.

Use one shared, chronological household projection engine for the full
household trajectory and goal feasibility. Dedicated investment-only,
debt-only, expenditure-only, and custom trajectories may filter or
specialize the same canonical data/calculation outputs, but must not use
inconsistent formulas.

4. CORE FINANCIAL RULES ALREADY AGREED
======================================================================

4.1 Goal priorities There are five ordered priority tiers: 1.
Non-negotiable 2. Very high 3. High 4. Not essential 5. Least essential

Within each tier, the user ranks individual goals. Allocate available
goal funding strictly in that ordered priority sequence, not
proportionally. Do not silently use money twice or fund lower-priority
goals while pretending a higher-priority goal has been satisfied.

Before discretionary goal funding, protect essential household expenses
and legally/contractually required payments. Emergency-fund
contributions follow the user’s selected plan while accounting for
immediate liquidity and mandatory obligations. Clearly show the
hierarchy and any user-configured exceptions.

If a high-priority goal is infeasible, say so plainly. Test alternatives
such as higher contributions, reduced expenditure, later deadline, lower
target, changed savings schedule, changes to lower-priority goals, or
alternative return assumptions. Recalculate each alternative. Classify
goals as feasible, feasible with adjustments, or currently unrealistic
under the tested assumptions. Never recommend a higher expected return
as if guaranteed.

4.2 Goal inflation User enters a goal amount, reason, deadline, priority
tier and within-tier order. Apply 5% annual goal inflation by default:
inflated target = entered target * (1.05 ^ number_of_years_to_goal) Show
the entered amount and inflation-adjusted amount. Let the user choose
which amount is the actual target used in the plan. Retain the original
amount, calculated amount, chosen amount, inflation assumption, date
basis and calculation timestamp. Clearly state whether the input
represents today’s money or a future nominal amount to avoid applying
inflation twice. Choose and document a consistent time convention
(e.g. exact date difference converted to fractional years or full-year
periods) and test it.

4.3 Expenditure inflation The user controls expenditure assumptions
independently of goal inflation. Allow: - expenses remain constant, -
one user-entered annual growth rate, - custom growth schedules by
period, - category-specific growth assumptions. Do not silently apply a
default expenditure inflation rate without disclosure. If a setup
default is required, explicitly label it and let the user choose/edit it
before a projection is treated as complete.

4.4 Income growth Each income source can have optional growth schedules.
If no growth is specified, hold the income amount constant for the
projection and disclose this assumption. Support variable frequency,
effective dates, optional end dates and irregular/variable income where
practical.

4.5 Emergency fund Default target is 12 months of expenditure, editable
by the user. The expenditure categories included in the baseline must be
explicit/configurable and documented. Track current emergency savings
separately from the target, projected contributions, shortfall and
expected completion date. Users may allocate eligible existing assets to
emergency reserves. Do not double-count allocated assets in net worth or
goal funding. Classify reserves by accessibility: - immediately
accessible cash/bank balances, - accessible with settlement time, -
restricted or costly-to-withdraw assets (e.g. FD with penalty or
lock-in). Show target, allocated amount, immediately accessible amount,
total eligible reserves, shortfall and completion estimate.

4.6 Physical assets Allow land/property and gold to be recorded as
assets. The user can specify if property is their residence; the user’s
chosen planning semantics treat the residence as not liquid. For other
property and gold, ask whether the user would sell it: Yes / No / Maybe.
“No” excludes it from available goal funding. “Maybe” excludes it from
committed base funding but can be evaluated in a separate scenario.
“Yes” can be considered available after estimated sale timing, costs,
applicable tax and secured liabilities are accounted for. Owned assets
remain part of net worth even when unavailable for goal funding.

4.7 Investments and expected returns Users enter expected annual returns
for each investment or relevant holding; do not silently assign generic
return assumptions. Contractual instrument terms override generic
assumptions where applicable. Keep estimated future returns separate
from actual observed performance. Support return schedules with multiple
date ranges per investment per scenario. Default rate change applies to
the entire holding from the effective date forward, including previously
invested money. Advanced users may model different assumptions for
existing holdings versus new contributions. Use chronological
compounding and clearly specify annual-to-periodic conversion and
day-count convention. No expected return is guaranteed.

4.8 SIP step-ups Support optional multi-step SIP schedules,
e.g. ₹20,000/month initially, ₹25,000/month from a specified date,
₹30,000/month later. Current amount applies until next schedule change
or SIP end date. Use the same schedule consistently in investment
trajectory, full household trajectory and goal feasibility. Distinguish
planned contribution from affordable contribution. Show yearly
contributions, cumulative invested amount, projected value, returns and
applicable taxes.

4.9 Multiple scenarios and tabs Users can create multiple named scenario
tabs, e.g. Baseline, Conservative, Optimistic or custom names. Each
scenario has independent assumptions and dated future events, including
investment return schedules, income changes, job loss, bonuses, medical
expenses, new loans, large purchases, investment gains/losses and
family-planning assumptions. Underlying financial records are shared;
scenario assumptions/events are isolated. Editing one scenario must not
mutate other scenarios. Support comparison of goal feasibility, net
worth, cash flow, debt-free dates, investment value and completion
dates. Save assumptions and calculation-run metadata so old comparisons
can be reproduced. User can compare scenarios side by side.

4.10 Taxes Tax rules must be explicit, versioned, auditable and
effective-date-aware. Research current Indian rules only when the user
enables external research. Use credible sources, record
URL/title/publisher/retrieval date/effective dates/financial year and
uncertainty. The AI may research or extract candidate rules, but
deterministic code applies validated rules. Support tax configuration
per relevant instrument and event; show pre-tax and post-tax projected
values where meaningful. Model tax when it affects cash flow: recurring
taxable income when applicable, taxable sale/redemption with acquisition
lots, cost basis and holding period where relevant, maturity tax on
taxable components, and post-tax proceeds reinvested only after tax
payment. Do not apply a simplistic flat rate to all asset classes.
Provide tax-base/type and instrument-specific logic (e.g. capital gains,
interest income, taxable maturity component, withholding/TDS, other
applicable base). Allow manual override with audit trail and disclose
estimates. Tax outputs are estimates, not tax/legal advice. Never imply
legal certainty where data/rules are incomplete. If exact rules cannot
be validated, clearly label the tax result as provisional and show the
missing data/assumptions. Include a rule-engine interface, not
hard-coded tax logic spread across modules.

4.11 Market data Use public market-data APIs only; no personal account
connections. Use a market-data adapter so providers can be changed. A
candidate user-proposed repository is
https://github.com/0xramm/Indian-Stock-Market-API; verify its current
endpoint docs, terms/licence, reliability and upstream data limitations
before use. Do not claim a licence if none is stated. Refresh prices on
portfolio open when cache is stale and provide manual refresh. Cache
results and avoid continuous streaming. Record instrument ID, exchange,
price, currency, source, market-data timestamp, retrieval timestamp and
stale/failure status. Preserve last known price if refresh fails and
visibly mark it stale. Stock APIs may cover supported stock/ETF quotes
and history; mutual-fund NAVs need a suitable NAV source
(e.g. official/public daily NAV data where available); FD, PPF, EPF,
NPS, private equity, property and many other assets require manual or
instrument-specific data. Separate actual current valuation from
projected future value.

4.12 CSV/XLSX import Users can import CSV/XLSX files but must review and
confirm rows before they affect official records or projections.
Workflow: 1. Upload and parse locally/backend securely. 2. Detect
columns and suggest mappings. 3. Gemini may suggest categories, detect
likely transfers, investments, repayments, refunds and recurring items,
and explain uncertain classifications. 4. Deterministic code validates
dates, amounts, duplicates, categories and transaction consistency. 5.
User reviews, edits, excludes and confirms rows. 6. Only confirmed rows
enter the canonical ledger and trigger recalculation. Transactions such
as SIP/MF purchases, insurance premiums, loan principal repayments and
internal transfers must be mapped to correct financial events, not
automatically treated as consumption expenditure. Store import batch,
source filename, mapping, row-level status, validation errors, duplicate
candidate links, review timestamps and confirmation state. Avoid storing
raw uploads longer than necessary. Do not silently import uncertain
rows.

4.13 Income sources No separate household-member management module is
required. Users can create any number of income sources in the Income
section. A partner’s contribution can be an additional source. Household
income totals are computed from selected income sources and schedules.

4.14 Trajectory views Support: - Household Trajectory: complete
household financial projection. - Investment Trajectory: selected/all
investment balances, contributions, returns, maturities, tax and
withdrawals without full household cash-flow forecast. - Debt
Trajectory: liabilities, interest, repayments, fees, outstanding
balances and debt-free dates, excluding insurance. - Expenditure
Trajectory: expense categories and growth assumptions over time. -
Custom Trajectory: user chooses eligible financial categories and
plus/minus components, a calculation type, time horizon and scenario.
Show row-by-row contributions and how each investment/component produces
the result. Include pre-tax and post-tax outputs where meaningful. -
Goal Feasibility: goal allocation, funding progress, shortfalls,
projected completion and trade-offs. - Scenario Comparison: compare
independent scenario tabs. Users can select a future year/date, inspect
month-by-month data, and export relevant results to CSV. For custom
trajectory calculation types support: (a) Net worth = assets minus
liabilities, with valuation and tax treatment appropriately identified.
(b) Net cash flow = inflows minus outflows over the selected period. (c)
Investment value = projected value of selected investments. (d) Custom
net result = a user-defined combination of eligible components, with
explicit signs and no category double-counting. Do not mix a
stock/balance metric with a period flow metric without a clear
definition. Clearly state what the final number represents. Before a
trajectory calculation, validate required fields specific to that
trajectory. Show missing-field checklist, why each field matters, direct
links to edit, and whether a disclosed estimate can be used. Do not
demand unrelated fields. Mark output as complete, estimated, or
incomplete and explain limitations.

4.15 Dashboard and navigation Use persistent sidebars on desktop and
compact/collapsible navigation on smaller screens. The main dashboard is
one comprehensive vertically scrolling financial command center. It must
not hide major metrics merely because there are many; organize sections
logically and provide jump navigation. Suggested order: 1. Overall
financial summary: net worth, total assets, liabilities, recent changes.
2. Cash flow: income, expenditure, savings, upcoming obligations and
available surplus. 3. Goals: priority, progress, deadlines, shortfalls,
feasibility. 4. Emergency fund: target, accessible reserves, shortfall,
completion. 5. Investments: holdings, valuation freshness, performance
and returns. 6. Debt/liabilities: outstanding balance, EMI, interest,
payoff. 7. Insurance: coverage, premiums, renewals, claims/maturity
events and gaps. 8. Tax overview: estimated liabilities, assumptions,
pre/post-tax projections. 9. Future financial trajectory. 10. Scenario
comparison. 11. Alerts/upcoming events. Use module-specific detail pages
for deeper records and charts. Avoid duplicated assets, liabilities or
flows across sections. Sidebar item “Financial Outlook” contains
Household Trajectory, Investment Trajectory, Debt Trajectory,
Expenditure Trajectory, Custom Trajectory, Goal Feasibility and Scenario
Comparison.

4.16 AI capabilities and boundaries Gemini must consider the user’s
complete relevant financial picture—not isolated modules—when analysing
risk capacity or suggesting actions. Its context should include all
available relevant information from profile, income, expenditure, goals,
assets, investments, liabilities, insurance, emergency reserves, tax
configuration, scenarios, dated events, and data quality/freshness. AI
responsibilities: - Explain projections and trade-offs in understandable
language. - Diagnose why goals are infeasible and identify contributing
constraints. - Suggest actionable changes to income, spending,
contribution schedules, deadlines, goal targets, debt repayment,
liquidity and scenarios. - Compare scenarios using deterministic engine
results. - Analyse household risk capacity using age/life stage,
dependants, parents, existing/planned children, household income
reliability, expenses, debts, emergency reserves, insurance, liquidity,
goal priorities and adverse-scenario sensitivity. - Optionally research
current Indian tax/financial rules and market context only when the user
enables external research. - Help classify CSV/XLSX transactions, but
never auto-confirm. - Identify missing/conflicting/stale data and ask
for clarification where necessary. AI must not: - Authoritatively
calculate returns, taxes, balances, debt schedules or goal feasibility
itself. - Invent data, assume future children/dependants as certain,
silently change records, or fabricate sources. - Treat risk tolerance as
risk capacity. - Recommend higher risk solely to make a goal
mathematically feasible. - Promise returns or guarantee a financial
outcome. - Make changes to records without user confirmation. - Ignore
higher-priority goals, essential expenses, required debt payments, or
scenario boundaries. The AI should receive a structured,
privacy-minimized snapshot rather than raw unnecessary personal data.
All recommendations must be grounded in current engine outputs, include
assumptions and explain trade-offs. Research-enabled mode is separate
from permission to use the user’s profile data.

4.17 Essential expense and obligation priority Default cash allocation
hierarchy: 1. Essential household expenses (housing, food, utilities,
essential transport, healthcare, etc.). 2. Legally/contractually
required payments (minimum debt repayments, statutory dues, other
unavoidable obligations). 3. Emergency-fund contributions per user’s
plan, considering immediate liquidity and mandatory obligations. 4.
Goals, strictly in the user’s ordered priority sequence. 5. Optional
spending and additional debt prepayments, optimized against preferences
and consequences. Handle overdue obligations, penalties, liquidity and
user-configured exceptions transparently. This is a planning hierarchy,
not a claim that every obligation has identical legal priority.

4.18 Debt Preserve detailed liability model from the reference PDF. It
covers home, vehicle, personal, education, credit card, BNPL/consumer,
business, gold, loan against assets, family/friend loans, tax/statutory
dues and other payables. Track appropriate fields such as lender,
sanctioned/disbursed amount, outstanding principal, interest rate,
fixed/floating type, EMI, tenure, next due date, fees, prepayment rules,
collateral and tax implications. Use quick entry for current balance/EMI
with progressive completion of details. Clearly mark limited projections
when interest rate, tenure or payment schedule is missing. Support
repayment strategy comparisons: - Avalanche: highest effective interest
cost first. - Snowball: smallest outstanding balance first. Respect
required minimum payments, fees, promotional rates, prepayment penalties
and secured obligations. Compare interest cost, payoff dates and goal
impact. Suggestions are not actual payment changes.

4.19 Insurance Preserve the reference PDF model including policies,
insured members/assets, coverage, premiums, payments, claims, nominees,
renewals, policy events, common policy fields and relevant insurance
types. Treat insurance as a full planning module: premiums, renewals,
maturity/payout events, employer-provided cover, coverage-gap detection,
expiring cover and user-editable coverage-needs estimates based on
income, liabilities, dependants and goals. Explain assumptions; don’t
present coverage estimates as definitive professional advice. Include
premium cash flows in household projections.

5. DATABASE SCHEMA — REQUIRED STARTING POINT
======================================================================

Inspect the attached reference PDF before finalizing the schema. The
following is a major schema blueprint, not permission to ignore useful
fields in the PDF. Produce complete tables with field names, types,
nullability, defaults, indexes, unique constraints, foreign keys,
relationships, enum values and rationale. Use appropriate
decimal/numeric types for money and rates; never use binary floating
point for stored currency. Include created_at, updated_at, ownership,
provenance and soft-delete/audit strategy where relevant. Do not put
every field in one enormous table. Use normalized related tables for
extensible financial instruments.

5.1 Identity and preferences - users: id, email/identity subject, auth
provider, status, created_at, updated_at, last_login_at. -
user_preferences: user_id, base_currency (INR default), timezone,
locale, date/number formats, dashboard preferences, default projection
horizon, privacy/research preferences. - household_profiles: id,
user_id, date_of_birth nullable, approximate_age nullable,
age_reference_date nullable, profile_completeness, created_at,
updated_at. - household_commitments: id, user_id, commitment_type
(parent_support, child, planned_child, other_dependant, family_event),
status/uncertainty, count, relationship_category, start_date,
expected_date, end_date/review_date, expected_amount, amount_frequency,
inflation/growth assumption, notes, provenance, last_confirmed_at. Avoid
storing unnecessary identifying details. - household_profile_answers:
user_id, question_key, answer_value/enum, uncertainty/declined status,
effective_date, provenance, updated_at. Use only if justified; don’t
duplicate normalized child/commitment records. -
household_risk_assessments: id, user_id, scenario_id nullable,
assessed_at, engine_version, input_snapshot_id, quantitative indicators
JSON or normalized results, qualitative findings,
confidence/limitations, explanation. Preserve transparency; don’t store
an opaque unexplained score only.

5.2 Income and expenditure - income_sources: id, user_id, name,
source_type, amount, currency, frequency, start_date, end_date nullable,
variable/reliability classification, include_in_household_projection,
source/provenance, notes. - income_growth_schedules: id,
income_source_id, effective_date, change_type (fixed_amount/percentage),
value, frequency, end_date nullable. - expense_categories: id, user_id
nullable for system defaults, name, category_group,
essential/discretionary classification, default inclusion flags. -
expense_schedules: id, user_id, category_id, description, amount,
currency, frequency, start_date, end_date, payment_day, essential flag,
growth_mode, source/provenance. - expense_growth_schedules: id,
expense_schedule_id, effective_date, change_type, value. -
cash_flow_events: id, user_id, scenario_id nullable, event_type, amount,
currency, event_date, recurrence, related_entity_type/id, status,
tax_treatment_ref, notes, source/provenance. Do not use generic
polymorphic references without integrity strategy; propose concrete FKs
or an event-subject mapping table. - transactions: id, user_id,
account_label (manual label only, not a connected account),
transaction_date, posted_date nullable, amount signed or debit/credit
with one consistent convention, currency, description,
merchant/counterparty optional, category_id, transaction_type,
transfer_group_id, related_instrument_id nullable, import_row_id
nullable, confirmation status, source, external_reference optional,
duplicate fingerprint, notes. - transaction_splits: id, transaction_id,
category_id, amount, related_entity type/reference, split purpose;
enforce sum-to-transaction consistency. - transfers: id, user_id,
transfer date, source/destination transaction IDs, amount, currency,
purpose; ensure transfers do not count as household income or
consumption twice. - recurring_transactions (optional if needed):
schedule, frequency, expected amount and related module; ensure actual
ledger entries do not double-count forecast entries.

5.3 Goals - financial_goals: id, user_id, name, reason, category,
original_target_amount, currency, goal_amount_basis
(today_money/future_nominal), deadline, target_inflation_rate default
0.05, inflation_method, calculated_inflated_amount,
selected_target_amount, selected_target_basis, priority_tier enum,

6. CALCULATION ENGINE AND FORMULA SPECIFICATION
======================================================================

Define a deterministic, chronological projection engine. Use monthly
periods as the primary default for household projections, but document
date handling and allow year-level summaries. Event dates should be
respected; do not treat all events as if they occur at year-end without
disclosure.

Specify: - Opening balances and period cash flows. - Income recurrence
and growth. - Expense recurrence and growth. - Essential vs
discretionary expenditure. - Loan amortization and rate changes. -
Investment contribution and return timing. - SIP step-up effective
dates. - Fees, distributions, withdrawals and taxes. - Asset sale dates,
sale costs and secured debts. - Maturities, lock-ins and liquidity. -
Emergency fund allocation and contribution. - Goal inflation and target
selection. - Priority-ordered funding and goal carry-forward. - Tax
event timing and post-tax reinvestment. - Scenario event processing and
precedence. - Handling missing fields, incomplete histories, stale
market data and negative cash flow. - Rounding, currency precision,
annual-to-monthly rate conversion, compounding convention and day-count
conventions. - Calculation reproducibility and versioning.

Explicitly distinguish: - balance-sheet values (net worth, asset value,
liability balance), - period flows (income, expenses, taxes paid,
contributions), - returns (investment gains/interest), - transfers (not
income/expense), - debt principal repayment (cash outflow and liability
reduction, not consumption expense), - investment purchases (cash
outflow and asset acquisition, not ordinary consumption), - insurance
premiums (cash outflow and policy-related expense/cash flow), - asset
sale proceeds (cash inflow and asset reduction; taxable gain is not
necessarily total proceeds), - taxes (actual event-based liability/cash
outflow, not universal end-of-projection deduction). Explain how each
affects net worth and cash flow to prevent double-counting.

Every formula needs a worked example and automated test cases for: -
regular and irregular income, - expenditure growth and no growth, - goal
inflation and target basis, - multiple goals in priority order, -
insufficient funding and carry-forward, - scenario return changes, - SIP
step-ups, - loan repayment and rate change, - avalanche vs snowball, -
emergency-fund allocation, - sale of asset with fees/tax/secured loan, -
taxable investment redemption, - imported transfer/repayment/investment
classification, - missing data and stale market data, - family
commitments and future-child uncertainty scenario, - pre-tax/post-tax
trajectory, - no double-counting of cash/investment/debt movements.

10. TEST STRATEGY
======================================================================

Specify unit, integration, API, UI and end-to-end tests. Prioritize
financial correctness and invariants: - Currency values use decimal
arithmetic. - Assets and liabilities are not double-counted. - Transfers
are not income/expenses. - Investment purchases are not consumption
expenses. - Loan principal repayments reduce debt and cash, but are not
ordinary consumption. - Scenario changes do not mutate other
scenarios. - Confirmed imports only affect official projections. -
Priority allocation does not fund lower goals before higher goals under
the agreed rules. - Asset allocations cannot exceed available value or
be reused. - Tax calculation references the rule version and effective
date. - Missing data is disclosed. - Market data freshness is visible. -
AI cannot override deterministic engine results. - All projections are
reproducible from saved inputs and versions. - User isolation prevents
cross-user data access. - Planned future family events remain uncertain
unless selected as assumptions. - Risk capacity considers whole
financial context and adverse scenarios. Provide test data fixtures and
expected outputs, not only test names.
